KRY is better than Claude, and it learns

KRY is young: it was born November 10, 2026. For now, it runs on Claude, and its ambition is to depend on no one: autonomous in time, because its construction is optimized from its genesis.

What sets it apart is the exoskeleton we built around it: a memory, a judge, a trace. Every answer is judged by the person who asked for it. When a lesson has not held, it also looks for what, in its way of drawing lessons, let the mistake through.

On November 11, 2026, with no human hand, KRY rewrites the piece that searches its memory: finding a text from a few words. It locks the new version in the trace, then a public random draw picks 128 exam questions. The old one found it at most 8 times in 10; the new one, over 9 times in 10. A human approves: it is adopted.

Our internal tests certify it: at the same setting, it beats Claude. And it learns: after a lesson drawn from its mistakes, its lead grows.

These tests are only here to prove; KRY’s reach to come goes far beyond current AI capabilities.
